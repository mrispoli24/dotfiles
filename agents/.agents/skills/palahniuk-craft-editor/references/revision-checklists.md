# Revision Checklists

Use these as targeted diagnostic passes. Do not run every checklist unless the user asks for a full deep edit.

## Fast Craft Triage

Ask these first:

- What is the intended effect?
- Where does the piece actually begin to get interesting?
- What is the strongest concrete detail?
- What is the most emotionally honest moment?
- What is the piece explaining that it could embody?
- What object, phrase, or setup can return at the end?
- What section has no job?
- Does the ending summarize, or does it land?

## Authority Checklist

- Does the piece establish heart authority, head authority, or both?
- Is the writer risking honesty, or hiding behind cleverness?
- Is there a concrete professional/lived detail that proves expertise?
- Does the opening make the writer look too polished too soon?
- Is there a vulnerable anecdote that should move earlier?
- Is there a specific example that should replace a broad claim?

## Thesis Opening Checklist

Flag openings that sound like:

- “The problem with...”
- “I’ve been thinking a lot about...”
- “In today’s world...”
- “The truth is...”
- “What people don’t understand is...”
- “This essay is about...”

Then ask:

- What image, scene, object, line of dialogue, or contradiction proves this point?
- Is the stronger opening hiding in paragraph two or three?
- Can the thesis move later?

## On-the-Body Checklist

Search for abstract emotion words:

- anxious
- afraid
- angry
- overwhelmed
- sad
- inspired
- disgusted
- tired
- stressed
- humiliated
- excited
- confused
- frustrated

Replace with:

- hands
- mouth
- breath
- stomach
- skin
- feet
- posture
- jaw
- shoulders
- sweat
- pulse
- silence
- gesture
- repeated action

Ask:

- What does this feeling make the body do?
- What would a camera see?
- What would a microphone hear?
- What would the person keep touching, refreshing, avoiding, carrying, or checking?

## Submerge the I Checklist

Search for:

- I think
- I feel
- I believe
- I realized
- I remember
- I noticed
- I want to say
- I have been thinking

Ask:

- Can this sentence stand without the “I”?
- Can the camera point to the room, client, object, product, or decision instead?
- Does this “I” create vulnerability or just throat-clearing?

## Horses Checklist

- What ideas repeat?
- What images repeat?
- Which repetitions feel intentional?
- Which repetitions feel accidental?
- Are there more than three competing metaphors?
- Does the ending return to the strongest horse?
- Does each recurrence add meaning or merely repeat the same point?

## Hidden Gun Checklist

Look for early:

- object
- deadline
- rule
- technical limitation
- promise
- lie
- phrase
- bodily injury
- process
- anecdote
- family detail
- joke
- number
- place

Ask:

- Does this return?
- Could it return?
- Would returning it create irony, payoff, reversal, or emotional closure?
- Is the setup too obvious?
- Is the payoff unsupported?

## Object Checklist

For every important object:

- What does it mean at first?
- Who touches it?
- Who wants it?
- Who avoids it?
- Does it pass between people?
- Does it change meaning?
- Does it become a memory cue, hidden gun, gesture prop, or symbol?
- Could it replace an explanatory paragraph?
- If it is not used, can it be cut?

## Dialogue Checklist

- Are people answering each other too directly?
- Does every question get an answer?
- Is anyone doing something with their hands?
- Does gesture contradict speech?
- Can a line be replaced by a physical beat?
- Are attributions clear enough when read aloud?
- Does each exchange shift power, reveal desire, or increase tension?

## Thought Verb Checklist

Search for:

- thought
- knew
- realized
- understood
- believed
- wanted
- remembered
- noticed
- wondered
- worried
- felt
- decided

Ask:

- What evidence would let the reader infer this?
- What action proves it?
- What object, gesture, or line of dialogue reveals it?

## Received Text Checklist

Search for stale or prebuilt phrases:

- at the end of the day
- elephant in the room
- deer in headlights
- heart skipped a beat
- pit in my stomach
- light at the end of the tunnel
- writing on the wall
- easier said than done
- only time will tell
- move fast and break things
- 10x engineer
- AI won't replace you, someone using AI will

Ask:

- Have I heard this phrase too many times?
- What is actually happening?
- How would this narrator say it?
- Can I use a concrete action instead?

## Simile Checklist

- Search for “like” and “as.”
- Does the comparison pull the reader out of the scene?
- Is the comparison generic?
- Does it reveal the narrator's life, job, class, place, or obsession?
- Would a metaphor be stronger?
- Can the sentence work without “like”?

## Texture Checklist

Would another form help?

- list
- memo
- email
- Slack thread
- bug report
- invoice
- script
- checklist
- transcript
- menu
- sign
- label
- warning
- contract clause
- calendar invite
- receipt

Use texture only if it:

- compresses time
- creates authority
- changes pacing
- reveals character
- makes the world feel real
- creates useful contrast

## Shape Checklist

Choose the shape that serves the piece:

- Straight line: useful for clarity, but often flat.
- O shape: start near crisis, then flash back.
- Thumbnail: preview the whole in miniature.
- Quilt: frame several stories/examples.
- Cycle: show one turn of a repeating system.
- Rebel/Follower/Witness: use competing extremes and a transformed observer.

Ask:

- Does the current structure create momentum?
- Is there a better opening later in the piece?
- Is this actually a system story?
- Is the narrator the main actor or the witness?

## Plot Point Checklist

For each section, name the job:

- setup
- payoff
- reversal
- authority
- embodied moment
- tension break
- time passage
- character reveal
- object transformation
- argument complication
- ending turn

Cut or revise sections with no job.

## Ending Checklist

- Does the ending return to the beginning?
- Does an object or phrase come back changed?
- Does the narrator act differently than they would have at the start?
- Is the final insight embodied rather than explained?
- Can the last explanatory paragraph be cut?
- Does the ending stop one beat before over-explaining?
