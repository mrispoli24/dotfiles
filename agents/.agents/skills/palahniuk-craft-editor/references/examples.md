# Examples

These examples show how to apply the craft principles without imitating Palahniuk's style.

## Thesis Opening to Specific Detail

Weak:

> AI is changing the way software teams think about engineering.

Stronger:

> The first version of the dashboard came from a product manager who had never opened VS Code before. By lunch, sales was using it. By Friday, legal wanted to know where the data lived.

Craft used: thesis opening replaced by concrete detail; head authority through real process; hidden gun planted with legal/data question.

## Abstract Emotion to On-the-Body

Weak:

> I was nervous before the client call.

Stronger:

> Ten minutes before the client call, the cursor hovered over the same refresh button. The coffee was still full. The mug had gone cold enough to sweat against my palm.

Craft used: on-the-body; object; gesture; no emotion label.

## Submerging the I

Weak:

> I realized that most teams do not actually have a speed problem. I think they have a feedback problem.

Stronger:

> Most teams do not have a speed problem. They have a feedback problem. The work moves faster than the room can understand it.

Craft used: submerging the I; direct claim; stronger rhythm.

## Hidden Gun in an Essay

Opening setup:

> The demo dashboard was supposed to be temporary.

Later payoff:

> Three months later, the temporary dashboard had a roadmap, a support queue, and one person in finance who threatened to quit if anyone took it away.

Craft used: hidden gun; object/process returning with greater force.

## Object as Argument

Weak:

> The company had a lot of legacy systems that needed modernization.

Stronger:

> The order system still printed to a dot-matrix printer in the shipping office. Nobody knew who bought the printer. Nobody knew where the replacement ribbons came from. But every invoice in the company still passed through that gray plastic box before a customer got paid.

Craft used: object; head authority; specificity; legacy modernization made physical.

## Gesture Instead of Dialogue

Weak:

> “Are you worried about the launch?”
> “Yes, I am worried.”

Stronger:

> “Are you worried about the launch?”
> Dana kept moving the same ticket from QA to Done and back again.
> “Who owns rollback?” she said.

Craft used: disconnected dialogue; gesture; subtext; C response.

## Cliché Replacement

Weak:

> The writing was on the wall.

Stronger:

> By then, nobody said the project was canceled. They just stopped inviting engineering to the meetings.

Craft used: received text replaced by concrete institutional behavior.

## Simile Improvement

Weak:

> The meeting was like a hostage situation.

Stronger:

> The meeting had the manners of a hostage situation: nobody blinked, nobody stood up, and every sentence sounded negotiated through a door.

Craft used: comparison unpacked; concrete behavior; voice.

## Texture of Information

Standard exposition:

> The team had too many communication channels and no one knew where decisions were made.

Texture version:

> The decision lived in Slack.
> The objection lived in a Google Doc comment.
> The deadline lived in Jira.
> The actual plan lived in someone's head, which is the worst project management tool ever invented.

Craft used: list; repetition; comedy; concrete systems.

## Cycle Shape for Business Essay

Cycle skeleton:

1. Company buys a “temporary” tool.
2. Everyone rationalizes the rough edges.
3. The tool becomes critical.
4. The person who built it leaves.
5. Nobody can change it.
6. Consultants are hired to modernize it.
7. The consultants build another “temporary” tool.

Craft used: cycle story; institutional trap; ending implies repetition.

## Rebel / Follower / Witness for Essay

Topic: AI in engineering.

- Rebel: the non-technical operator building tools without permission.
- Follower: the engineering team clinging to process and control.
- Witness: the product leader who sees both sides and realizes the company needs a new contract between speed and responsibility.

Craft used: mythic role map; nuance beyond binary argument.

## Ending That Returns

Opening:

> The dashboard was built in a weekend.

Weak ending:

> This shows why companies need to think more deeply about AI and engineering.

Stronger ending:

> The dashboard was still running Monday morning. So was the meeting.

Craft used: return to object; changed meaning; ending before explanation.
